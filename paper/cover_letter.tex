\emph{IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing}

\textbf{Re: Submission of the regular paper ``What Can Be Enforced? Limits of Runtime
Guardrails for Tool-Using LLM Agents''}

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as a regular paper in
\emph{IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing}.

Tool-using language-model agents send email, move money, and edit files while
reading untrusted content, and deployments increasingly rely on a runtime guardrail,
a judge-driven gate in front of each tool call, as the security boundary against
prompt injection and misuse. Guardrail evaluations report violation rates, but not
which policies a gate can enforce, what a calibrated certificate guarantees, or when
that guarantee fails. The manuscript answers these questions with three boundaries,
each stated with the regime in which it holds, and tests their empirical
consequences.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.25em}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item \emph{Expressiveness.} A model of pre-execution gates over irreversible,
data-carrying tool calls that exactly characterizes the policies enforceable relative
to fixed oracle predicates: the nonempty safety policies whose good prefixes the
gate's register automaton recognizes, strictly fewer than edit automata enforce.
\item \emph{Statistics, adversaries, and feedback.} For fallible judges: for a fixed exogenous decision,
the exact Neyman--Pearson miss/false-block frontier, a finite-sample conformal certificate and
the robustness margin that bounded attacks on judge inputs add to it, and a proof that
static scores and ungated trajectories cannot identify the closed-loop frontier once
blocks change later proposals, whereas logs with known exploratory blocking can, with an
occupancy program for specified finite models.
\item \emph{Decidability.} Whether a policy can ever fire is undecidable with two
decrementable, zero-testable counters, PSPACE-complete for a keyed-counter fragment, and
NP-complete for caps, quotas, and revocation (no data registers, amount tests that are cap checks), so
a complete linter for them is one SMT query.
\item \emph{Evaluation.} A study of 33 judge runs on AgentDojo instruction strings; an in-context
study in which seven hosted judges score ground-truth tool calls with and without the user task
and tool outputs; two prompt-injection detector baselines; representation attacks (a predeclared
suffix, query-adaptive rewrites, and an optimizing score-feedback attacker, on instruction strings
and inside the injection itself); closed-loop agent runs; and the cost of exact enforcement. What
the judge sees dominates: in context the judges reach AUC .950--1.000 and calibrated gates block
at most 5.3\% of clean compliant calls, against .661--.842 and 44--93\% for the same calls
serialized alone. Context buys discrimination, not verified robustness: rewriting only the injection evades
those gates stably on up to 12\% of flagged calls, and an optimizing attacker evades even the expensive
instruction-string gates on 27--35\% of flagged violations. The best context-free gate calibrated on 17--18
violations blocks 64\% of compliant instructions (95\% CI 20--83\%; 33\% with exact-level
randomized calibration). A compiled keyed-counter gate decides in a sub-microsecond median, about
six orders of magnitude faster than one hosted judge call.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Fit with TDSC.} The paper treats a guardrail as a dependability mechanism:
in the taxonomy of Avi\v{z}ienis, Laprie, Randell, and Landwehr (TDSC, 2004), the gate
contains errors before they become irreversible failures, misses are security and
safety failures, and false blocks are omission failures that cost availability. The
results combine runtime enforcement and security automata, statistical guarantees
for fallible detectors, adversarial robustness of those detectors, and decidability
of policy analysis, all topics central to this journal, and end in concrete
guidance for practitioners, including a ``certificate card'' that states what any
guardrail evaluation must report before its numbers can support a security claim.

\textbf{Prior version.} An earlier version of this work is available as
arXiv:2607.22868 (July 24, 2026, ``What Can Be Enforced? A Theory of Certified
Runtime Safety for Tool-Using Agents''). The manuscript has not been
published and is not under consideration elsewhere. Relative to the preprint, the
submission
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
\item is retitled and restructured for the journal, with every result and headline number
in the main text and complete proofs, the full evaluation, and extended related work in the
supplementary material;
\item unifies the evaluation into one study of 33 judge runs that adds late-2026
frontier judges, familywise (max-$T$) inference against a predeclared reference
judge, and a content-benign sensitivity analysis;
\item re-analyzes the representation attack over all 35 violations for 21 judges with
simultaneous intervals, extends the template search to the new judges, and adds a
predeclared query-adaptive search, both measured at a high-utility flagged-subclass
threshold;
\item adds a converse to the non-identifiability result: logs from a gate that both blocks
and allows with known probabilities of at least $\varepsilon$ at every reachable history
restore identification of the closed-loop frontier, at a variance cost that can grow
exponentially in the horizon, checked by simulation on the two-system model;
\item adds a matching PSPACE lower bound (one equality-register component suffices, by the
standard register-automaton argument of Demri and Lazi\'c), so nontriviality of the keyed-counter fragment is
PSPACE-complete; proves that for caps, quotas, rate limits, and revocation (no data registers,
cap-check amount tests) nontriviality is NP-complete, decidable by one existential Presburger (SMT) query, and that
dropping the cap-check condition restores PSPACE-hardness; and shows that rule redundancy (policy inclusion) is
PSPACE-complete when the allowing side has no key-local component, and coNP-complete for these cap-check rules;
\item adds an in-context tool-call evaluation on AgentDojo ground-truth traces (with a context
ablation and a tagless-injection variant), query-adaptive attacks on the in-context gates, a
predeclared optimizing (PAIR-style) attacker on the calibrated gates, and prompt-injection detector
baselines (ProtectAI DeBERTa-v3, PIGuard);
\item adds a guardrail certificate card with red flags and a verdict, a fill-in template and JSON
schema in the artifact, and the card's fields for all 33 judge runs (Supplement~D);
\item adds a new experiment on the cost of exact enforcement and specification
analysis (compiled gate latency, a differential check against a naive replay monitor,
hosted-judge latency, and a cap-redundancy check built on the T3 saturation
abstraction);
\item adds the dependability and reference-monitor framing and related work updated through 2026, including work published in this journal.
\end{itemize}
